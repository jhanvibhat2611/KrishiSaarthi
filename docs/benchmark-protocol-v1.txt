Phase 6 benchmark protocol v1 — pre-specified draft for human approval

Status and scope
No new data approved or labeled; no new retrieval method or router implemented.
Freeze the current fourteen lexical/dense/hybrid configurations as comparison
configurations. The original 16 signed needs and 24 pending stress variants are
pilot/development only, never the independent final test. Prior Phase 5 hypotheses
are historical proposals, not confirmed contributions. This document governs the
next benchmark only after approval and version/hash registration before test access.

Collection and split
First author a development batch of 20 needs after new source regions are approved.
Proposed composition: 16 answerable, four corpus-relative unanswerable; six of the
answerable needs require ALL of at least two spans. These are recruitment targets,
not prefilled judgments. Target at least three documents and three applicable
regions, no more than eight needs from one document. If approved evidence cannot
support that balance, report the gap; never manufacture examples to fill quotas.

Later retain the proposed larger target: 60 development answerable +15 unanswerable
and 120 independent held-out answerable +30 unanswerable, each with English, Hindi
and natural Hinglish. The first 20 belong inside development, not in addition to a
new hidden test. At least 40 held-out answerable needs should require multiple spans.
These targets do not establish statistical power. Assess precision with development
paired disagreements before sealing test size; never resize after viewing outcomes.

Before questions are assigned, cluster evidence families across documents: repeated
recommendations, cultivar facts, conditions, copied passages, nearby editions and
connected multi-span requirements. All related questions, translations, stress
variants and connected evidence families stay in one split. If a need crosses two
families, merge their split blocks or exclude that need. Do not split by passage ID
or random query row. Prefer document/section holdouts too, while keeping topics and
regions represented. Corpus may include all approved evidence as unlabeled retrieval
material; test questions, labels and judgments stay hidden from method development.
Vocabulary/IDF computed from the indexed collection is allowed; test-question or
label-derived rules, lexicons or choices are not. The pilot families are development
blocks even if the same statements occur in new documents.

Baseline matrix (both E5 and BGE-M3 backbones)
1 Original BM25; 2 punctuation-filtered BM25.
3 E5 dense-only; 4 BGE-M3 dense-only.
5–6 original equal-weight standard RRF.
7–8 original BM25 positive-score-gated RRF.
9–10 punctuation-filtered standard RRF.
11–12 punctuation-filtered positive-score-gated RRF.
13–14 content-token-gated diagnostic (current policy, including numeric tokens).
No old configuration is replaced. k1=1.5, b=.75, epsilon=.25; RRF k=60, weights 1/1;
no tuning. Preserve pinned models, tokenizer revisions, inference settings, normalized
embeddings, stable passage-ID tie-breaking and strict no-truncation checks.

Routing comparators, proposed only and explicitly specified before implementation:
For each dense backbone, supplied-language router: hi -> dense-only; en/hinglish ->
filtered positive-score RRF; missing/unknown tag -> dense-only. A supplied human
language tag is operational metadata, not a predicted language detector; report this
assumption. Do not use answerability, evidence IDs or gold overlap.
Script router: over Unicode letters only, >50% Devanagari -> dense-only; otherwise
filtered positive-score RRF; no letters -> dense-only. Script identifies script,
not English versus Hinglish. This fixes the stronger filtered-gated alternate branch
rather than the original-standard branch suggested in the historical Phase 5 plan;
it is a declared new protocol proposal, not a retroactive change to that experiment.
No trained language detector or threshold selection. Future adaptive method requires
separate architecture approval and pre-specified development selection.

Ranking depth and evidence
Keep 256-token chunks with 48 overlap, reviewed boundaries and source hashes.
Use complete collection rankings for each component in this modest benchmark before
selecting Top-K, just as in the pilot. No silent top-100 cutoff. If scale makes that
impractical, obtain approval for a separately versioned equal-budget protocol before
running the held-out test. Record ALL evidence spans; strict full canonical interval
coverage by the union of same-source/version retrieved passages. One span counts once.
No minimal-answer collapse or score-dependent gold relabeling.

Primary endpoint — group-macro complete evidence coverage@3
For answerable need g and its three approved base language variants l, C(g,l,K)=1
only if ALL required gold spans are completely covered in Top-K; otherwise 0.
Primary = mean over answerable groups of [C(g,en,3)+C(g,hi,3)+C(g,hinglish,3)]/3.
Report each language separately as mean_g C(g,l,3). Require all three reviewed base
variants; missing language is a validation failure, not a smaller denominator.
Exclude explicitly signed unanswerable needs from positive-evidence metrics; retain
counts/reasons and ranked results. Missing labels are errors, not unanswerability.
Stress variants receive a separate group-macro table by perturbation type. They do
not change the primary three-language denominator or add independent samples.
This is a specified report aggregation over the existing per-query metrics. Do not
change the current evaluation runner or original macro metrics silently. A future
separate group-aggregation report needs tests before use; it is not implemented here.

Secondary endpoints
Unchanged Recall@1/3/5, cumulative_evidence_mrr_at_k at 1/3/5 and complete coverage@1/5,
aggregated with the same base-group/language weights. Keep cumulative evidence MRR's
first fully covered ANY span semantics; it does not measure complete multi-span
retrieval. Reserve mrr_at_k for conventional judged passage relevance.
Per-language failure rate = answerable queries with incomplete ALL coverage at K
/ answerable queries of that language. Also report zero-recall failures, missing-span
IDs and multi-span subset. Report lexical no-positive rates separately over ALL
queries, including unanswerables; never call cosine scores lexical matches. No claim
that retrieval abstains on unanswerable needs without a separately approved policy.
Runtime: separate cold model loading, corpus encoding/indexing, warm query encoding,
search and fusion; hardware/device, batch size, cache state, token lengths, p50/p95
and peak memory. Use identical declared query order and three measured warm passes
with a fixed warm-up, without interleaved model loading. Saved-ranking replay runtime
cannot be compared with end-to-end dense inference runtime.

Paired analysis and uncertainty
Pair systems on identical groups and corpus versions. Primary pre-specified contrasts,
for each backbone: filtered positive-score RRF versus dense-only, versus original
standard RRF, versus filtered standard RRF; content-gated versus filtered-positive;
filtered-positive versus supplied-language router and versus script router. Twelve
contrasts total. Other matrix comparisons are descriptive unless added before test
unblinding. Always report absolute deltas and per-group wins/ties/losses.
Resample evidence-family blocks, keeping all grouped needs and languages together,
10,000 paired bootstrap replicates, seed 20261101. Recompute group-weighted estimates
within each replicate; report percentile 95% paired intervals and cluster counts.
Report secondary language and multi-span intervals as exploratory. Do not treat 3
translations or many overlapping chunks as independent observations. For sparse
family counts, say intervals are unstable; include document-level sensitivity rather
than claiming many query variants solve limited source diversity.
If hypothesis testing is later warranted and approved, preregister a paired family-
level permutation test and Holm correction over the twelve primary contrasts; do
not infer significance from uncorrected intervals or this tiny development batch.
No held-out tuning, best-backbone selection, query exclusion or threshold search.
Unblind once after corpus, labels, code, baselines, seed and protocol hashes freeze.
Any post-test change is exploratory and needs a fresh test set for confirmation.

Ablations and acceptance boundary
Use the existing factorial controls: original/filtered tokens; all/positive lexical
contributions; positive/content gate; dense-only; supplied-language/script routing.
Hold candidate depth, labels, k and encoders fixed. Inspect entity/numeric, script,
keyword-retention and spelling strata, defined before result viewing. Do not
introduce another lexical heuristic to fix one observed query. Negative results are
valid. A future proposed method must improve beyond the strong controls on independent
needs with uncertainty reported; the current pilot cannot establish novelty.

Annotation and release gates
Follow docs/development-annotation-workflow.txt. Actual independent author/reviewer
identities, review timestamps and adjudication are required; do not fabricate them.
Unknown licenses stay unknown. Publish no raw documents, passages, filled annotations
or generated outputs without explicit rights review. Source/corpus/annotation/model/
configuration and experiment fingerprints must be retained locally for reproducibility.

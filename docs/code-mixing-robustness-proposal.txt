PROPOSAL ONLY — not implemented or labeled

Question: How does lexical overlap and script choice affect multilingual dense
retrieval and equal-weight RRF, and how much harmful fusion is attributable to
non-positive BM25 ranks rather than positive but misleading lexical scores?

Design
Use the same frozen evidence collection initially, without modifying the signed
48-query baseline. Create a separate versioned, human-reviewed challenge set.
For each of the 12 answerable information needs, propose a matched set of:
(1) natural Hinglish retaining English agricultural terms;
(2) equivalent Hinglish using Hindi terminology with few/no English keywords;
(3) a natural Romanized-Hindi spelling variation of (2);
(4) a Hindi-script rendering of (2).
Keep crop, cultivation, dates, units and AND/multi-span conditions identical.
Human reviewers verify semantic equivalence and evidence sufficiency before any
labels are exported. Four unanswerable needs may receive analogous variants but
stay excluded from evidence-retrieval metrics. Do not claim retrieval rankings
alone establish an answerability detector. This is a separate robustness dataset,
not an overwrite or expansion of the signed baseline without approval.

Overlap measurement and controls
Pre-register a Unicode-tokenizer-based overlap ratio: distinct query terms that
also occur in the union of its required evidence passages divided by distinct
query terms. Separately count corpus-vocabulary overlap and English-origin
agricultural terms using human annotation: Latin script is not synonymous with
English. Record stopwords, numbers and punctuation separately to distinguish
agricultural matches from incidental matches. Define high versus low overlap as
a within-information-need paired contrast, avoiding thresholds chosen from
retrieval outcomes. Preserve naturalness; report pairs that cannot be matched
without changing meaning rather than forcing them. Script comparison should
use reviewed transliterations with otherwise matched wording, and explicitly
report confounding differences that remain.

Frozen comparisons
Run BM25, E5, BGE-M3 and the four fixed RRF variants using the same corpus,
model revisions, constant 60 and evaluation definitions. No normalization,
translation, reranking, training or hyperparameter search is proposed here.

Primary outcomes
Paired change in complete evidence coverage@3 (primary), with Recall@1/3/5,
cumulative evidence MRR@1/3/5 and complete coverage@1/5 as secondary outcomes.
Report per-information-need effects, worst-variant complete coverage and variation
within each matched set. Language variants from one need are not independent
samples. Keep IN-08 and IN-10 ALL-required and inspect each span's rank.

Fusion mechanism diagnostics
Count BM25 queries with no positive scores, all-zero lists, partial positive lists,
and positive matches consisting only of incidental tokens. Compare standard vs
gated RRF against the same dense input: number of dense successes lost, number
recovered by gating, and residual failures with positive but misleading matches.
When every BM25 score is non-positive, gated ordering must exactly equal dense
ordering (an invariant, not empirical proof of a new method). Separate cutoff
crossings caused by deterministic zero-score ordering from meaningful lexical
retrieval. Do not threshold cosine/RRF scores as lexical matches.

Potential measurable contribution
A human-validated matched multilingual/code-mixed challenge set could quantify
within-need robustness loss under reduced English keyword overlap, spelling and
script changes, and isolate which fusion failures are explained by zero-score
rank contributions. Report effect sizes and raw paired outcomes, including null
and adverse effects. RRF and score gating themselves are established baselines,
not the contribution. The tiny single-document pilot is hypothesis-generating;
any general claim needs independently reviewed, held-out needs and broader
sources approved separately. No significance claim is justified by this pilot.
